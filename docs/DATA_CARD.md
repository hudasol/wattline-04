## Site and quantity

One house in Sceaux, 7 km south of Paris. The raw field used is `Global_active_power`, the
household's minute-averaged active power in **kW**. wattline's target is the energy in each clock
hour, **kWh** = mean of the present minutes' kW × 1 h.

Unit checks at the source (so the unit is verified, not assumed):

- active power ≤ apparent power (`Voltage × Global_intensity / 1000`) on **94.4 %** of minutes;
  median power factor 0.99 — consistent with kW vs kVA;
- the three sub-meters (Wh per minute) never exceed total active energy (`kW × 1000 / 60` Wh) on
  **99.998 %** of minutes — consistent with kW;
- hourly values span 0.12–6.6 kWh, plausible for a French domestic supply.

## Timezone and DST — what the file really is

The file has naive `dd/mm/yyyy` and `hh:mm:ss` columns and no declared zone. Findings:

| check | result |
|---|---|
| rows on each of the 8 DST-transition dates | exactly 1440 every time (no jump, no repeat) |
| intraday **grid-voltage** profile shift across each transition (logger time) | **0 min** at all 8 (a clock ignoring DST would show ~60 min; ±60 min fits 2× worse) |
| spring-forward 02:00–02:59 rows | **exact copies** of 03:00–03:59 (59/60 rows identical in every column), all 4 years |

Voltage is driven by the whole local network's load, i.e. by thousands of households on the
legal wall clock, so it is a reliable clock witness. Conclusion: the logger ran on
**Europe/Paris wall-clock time** and the publisher re-gridded the file to 1440 rows/day, filling
the non-existent spring hour with a copy and keeping only one of the two autumn 02:00 hours.

Policy (`wattline.ingest.logger_to_utc`): spring copies are **dropped** (240 rows); the single
autumn 02:00–02:59 hour cannot be attributed to either occurrence, so it is **dropped** (240 rows)
and both UTC hours become missing. 480 rows dropped, nothing shifted. Canonical timestamps are
UTC interval starts; Europe/Paris is derived only for calendar features and display.

Missing-value policy for model **inputs** (`wattline.clean`, causal, train-only): a missing hour
takes the already-filled value from 168 h earlier, or the train-only hour-of-week median if that is
before the series start. All 442 imputations in train+validation used the 168-h rule. Imputed
values are never targets; an origin is not scored if > 25 % of its previous 168 h were missing
(31 train and 8 validation origins excluded, listed in `results/validation/selection.json`).

## Biases and limits of this data

- **One household.** Results say nothing about commercial buildings, other climates, other
  occupants or other countries. A "facility manager" framing is aspirational; this is a home.
- **Occupancy regimes are invisible.** August vacancies (2009, 2010) and holidays change the load
  completely and are not labelled anywhere.
- **Heating/appliances are unknown.** Winter load is higher (electric heating or water heating on
  a night tariff is likely — the 02:00 water-heater signature on sub-meter 3), but no weather or
  tariff data is used.
- **Re-gridded timestamps.** The publisher altered the DST hours; other undocumented
  re-processing is possible.
- **2006–2010 era.** No heat pump, EV or PV — modern load shapes differ.

## Intended use

Teaching and research on leakage-safe day-ahead forecasting for one site. Not for billing,
control, dispatch or any decision about the occupants.
